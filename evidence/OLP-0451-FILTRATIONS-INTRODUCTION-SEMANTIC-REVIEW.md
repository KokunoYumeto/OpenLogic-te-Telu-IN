## Reverse reading

1. The opening distinguishes an effective decision procedure from finite countermodel enumeration. For a fixed frame on $n$ worlds, each occurring propositional variable has $2^n$ valuations; for $m$ occurring variables there are $2^{nm}$ assignments. There are also $2^{n^2}$ binary accessibility relations on the world set. The translated paragraph keeps these counts and the fixed-frame qualification.
2. Enumerating finite models semidecides *nonvalidity*: a finite countermodel eventually appears, but a valid formula or one with only infinite countermodels would not stop that search. The finite model property rules out the second obstacle, while a formula-dependent upper size bound is still needed for this search itself to decide validity. The target does not conflate these two steps.
3. The quotient strategy identifies worlds that agree on relevant formulas. It requires finitely many equivalence classes, not infinite size of every class. `OLTENMLFILINT-001` records and discloses this correction to the English sentence. A class may be finite or infinite, and the quotient world $[w]$ must preserve the truth of $!A$ at $w$.
4. The toy modal step is read with universal accessibility. Then $\Box !B$ holds at a world precisely when $!B$ holds at every world. This makes the later universal Box induction coherent. `OLTENMLFILINT-002` records the source's incompatible no-accessibility/existential-Box wording and the corrected clause, including the exact formula delta.
5. The initial equivalence compares the finitely many variables occurring in $!A$, rather than every variable of the possibly infinite model. `OLTENMLFILINT-003` discloses that restriction. 6. The negation step uses the induction hypothesis at the same representative class. In the universal-accessibility toy case the displayed Box direction goes from every quotient class satisfying $!B$ to every original world satisfying $!B$, hence to $\Box !B$ at $w$. The target does not imply that this toy construction alone handles arbitrary accessibility.
