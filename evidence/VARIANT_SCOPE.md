# Telugu edition form and variant scope

Status: evidence-bounded recommendation, prospectively revisable

Locale and script: Telugu, Telugu script, India (`te-Telu-IN`).

## Recommended release scope

Publish one configurable-independent edition in standard formal Telugu written
in Telugu script. Preserve Open Logic's Arabic decimal digits, Latin
metavariables, logical symbols and left-to-right mathematical layout exactly.
Do not create a second script, transliterated Roman edition, separate Andhra
Pradesh/Telangana edition, Telugu-digit edition, or separate colloquial
register at the present evidence threshold.

## Evidence

The recorded canon contains native Telugu educational witnesses from both
Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, plus pre-bifurcation material now hosted in
Telangana. TE-C001 and TE-C003 are Telangana witnesses; TE-C002 is a 2024
Andhra Pradesh bilingual mathematics text whose Telugu pages, not its English
facing pages, are evidence; TE-C004 is a 2012 Andhra Pradesh Open School text
with a pre-bifurcation lineage. The seventeen visually checked passages use
Telugu script for mathematical exposition and share a broadly compatible
formal proof/logic register. They do show local vocabulary choices and one
different natural-number convention, both handled in the decision ledger;
they do not establish a systematic script, orthography or regional-variety
split that would justify duplicated editions.

The Open Logic source itself controls formulas, metavariables, numerals,
references and proof-tree layout. Retaining those notational forms supports
source parity, reproducible QA and cross-language comparison. Telugu prose is
left-to-right, so no bidirectional math layer is needed. Named formalisms such
as టాబ్లో, సీక్వెంట్, ఐగెన్, కట్ and మోడస్ పోనెన్స్ are explicit, reviewable
borrowings rather than evidence for a second script.

## Tradeoffs and review trigger

A single edition avoids doubling 722-unit structural and semantic QA while the
translation is incomplete. It also prevents an unevidenced regional split
from being mistaken for a linguistic requirement. The cost is that some
readers may prefer different technical synonyms or a more colloquial teaching
register; those choices remain visible and reversible in the expert-review
index.

This recommendation is not a claim that all Andhra Pradesh, Telangana,
diaspora, accessibility or classroom usage has been surveyed. Reconsider it
if native subject experts or authoritative curricula provide evidence of a
systematic, audience-relevant difference that cannot be served by glossary
aliases or ordinary editorial revision. Any future variant must state its
audience, source basis, exact differences, compatibility impact and whether a
separate build is genuinely necessary.
