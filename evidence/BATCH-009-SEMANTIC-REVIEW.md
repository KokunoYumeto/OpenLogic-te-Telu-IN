## Source-aligned checks and reverse paraphrases

- OLP-0063 retains both tag-controlled chapter identities and the exact import
  order: introduction, sequent calculus, natural deduction, tableaux and
  axiomatic deduction. The editorial still explains that a textbook may pair
  the common introduction with the relevant survey section when introducing a
  particular system. Reverse paraphrase: this wrapper gathers shared proof-
  system material and does not itself add a proof rule or duplicate an
  imported chapter.
- OLP-0064 preserves the syntax/semantics contrast and the mechanical
  checkability requirement for finite derivations. It retains the historical
  progression from axiom systems to natural deduction, truth trees/tableaux,
  sequent calculus and resolution, together with the sequence-versus-tree
  distinction. The two displayed equivalences still require theoremhood to
  match validity and derivability to match entailment. The Telugu wording
  explicitly identifies soundness as the derivability-to-entailment direction
  and completeness as the converse. The final consistency discussion retains
  the syntactic definition by unrestricted derivability, its semantic
  counterpart in satisfiability, and the corresponding directions of
  soundness and completeness. Reverse paraphrase: a useful proof system must
  never derive a semantic non-consequence; a complete one must also derive
  every semantic consequence, and in classical first-order logic this
  correspondence aligns syntactic consistency with satisfiability.
- OLP-0065 preserves sequents as pairs of independently sized sentence
  sequences, either of which may be empty, and retains the tree orientation,
  initial sequents and rule types. The sample left-conjunction and
  right-conditional inferences remain exact. Derivability still requires a
  finite premise sequence drawn from Gamma at the root, theoremhood still uses
  an empty antecedent, and inconsistency still uses an empty succedent. The
  displayed proof of `(A and B) implies A`, weakening consequence and Gentzen
  history are retained. Reverse paraphrase: a sequent-calculus derivation is a
  finite rule tree whose root expresses the target consequence.
  OLTEPRF-001 replaces the right sequence's final subscript m by n in the
  purported general sequent, making its length independent as the immediately
  following definition requires.
- OLP-0066 retains the intended correspondence between natural deduction and
  ordinary mathematical reasoning: proof by cases, indirect proof and
  conditional proof, plus introduction and elimination rules for connectives
  and quantifiers. It preserves the tree of formulas, temporary assumptions,
  discharge tracking, undischarged-premise condition for derivability and
  all-discharged condition for theoremhood. The sample derivation still
  eliminates conjunction and then introduces a conditional while discharging
  assumption 1. Inconsistency remains derivability of falsehood, and the
  historical and proof-theoretic-semantics discussion is retained. Reverse
  paraphrase: a natural-deduction proof may reason under temporary hypotheses,
  but only its undischarged leaves count as premises of the final consequence.
- OLP-0067 preserves signed formulas as truth-sign/sentence pairs and tableaux
  as downward-branching trees generated by rules. It retains the paired rules
  for signs, the true- and false-conjunction behavior, branch closure by a
  matching true/false pair, and derivability via a closed tableau whose initial
  assumptions contain false A and true finite premises. The repaired example
  still begins with false `((A and B) implies A)`, expands the false
  conditional, decomposes the resulting true conjunction, and closes on A.
  The history, systematic construction, model read-off from a saturated open
  branch and upside-down connection to sequent calculus are all retained.
  Reverse paraphrase: a closed tableau refutes the possibility that every
  selected premise is true while the conclusion is false. OLTEPRF-002 supplies
  only the conjunction operator to the documented rule-label macro;
  OLTEPRF-003 labels both children of the true conjunction with the
  true-conjunction rule rather than the unrelated true-conditional rule; and
  OLTEPRF-004 requires every displayed finite premise to belong to Gamma, not
  merely some one of them.
- OLP-0068 preserves axiomatic derivations as sentence sequences in which each
  line is an axiom, a member of Gamma or justified by an inference rule. It
  retains axiom schemata, soundness/completeness, alternative primitive
  signatures, single-axiom systems and the modus-ponens condition. The
  definition of theoremhood still sets Gamma empty. The three-line example,
  both schema instantiations and the modus-ponens identification of C and D
  remain exact. Inconsistency still means derivability of falsehood, with
  explosion supplied by a complete axiom system. The Frege, Whitehead/Russell
  and Hilbert history and the tradeoff between metatheoretic simplicity and
  practical proof construction are retained. Reverse paraphrase: axiomatic
  proofs are easy to specify and study because only schemas and a few rules
  govern a linear sequence, although finding such proofs can be difficult.

## Canon use and limits

TE-P002 supports native logic, mathematical-statement and truth/falsity
register. TE-P003 directly supplies reasoning, proof, induction and deduction
language. TE-P004 supports theorem, proof and uniqueness register. None of
these pages independently attests formal derivability, soundness,
completeness, consistency, sequents, natural-deduction rule classes,
assumption discharge, tableaux, signed formulas, axiom schemata, modus ponens,
resolution or proof-theoretic semantics. TE-T034 and TE-T035 therefore record
the grouped choices as provisional. Sequent, tableau, modus ponens and
resolution are explicit borrowings; all formal extensions are fixed by the
adjacent OpenLogic definitions, rule displays and derivations rather than
overstated witness authority.
