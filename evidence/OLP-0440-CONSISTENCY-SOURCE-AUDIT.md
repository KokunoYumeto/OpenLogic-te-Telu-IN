1. Lines 15–17 say an inconsistent set's formulas cannot all be true “in a model at a world” without qualifying the model. With consistency relative to an arbitrary modal system $\Sigma$, the soundness implication applies only in a class of models validating that system. Concrete countercheck: $\Sigma=\Log{KT}$ proves $\Box p\lif p$, so $\{\lnot(\Box p\lif p)\}$ is $\Sigma$-inconsistent; yet its sole formula is true at a world that sees a $p$-world but itself falsifies $p$ (a nonreflexive model). The Telugu explanation restricts this claim to models for which the system is sound, and discloses the qualification as OLTENMLAXSCON-001. This does not change the later canonical-model completeness claim, which concerns a model of the given system.

Lines 53–55 infer $\Gamma,!A\Proves[\Sigma]\lfalse$ and $\Gamma,\lnot !A\Proves[\Sigma]\lfalse$ from the non-consistency of the corresponding unions, but attribute this immediate inference to `prop:consistencyfacts-b`. It follows directly from the just-stated definition of $\Sigma$-consistency plus the union shorthand of OLP-0439; item (b) instead relates $\Gamma\Proves[\Sigma]!A$ to the inconsistency of $\Gamma\cup\{\lnot !A\}$. The Telugu proof retains the mathematical lines and the source reference as contextual background, but names the definition as the direct basis and discloses OLTENMLAXSCON-002.
